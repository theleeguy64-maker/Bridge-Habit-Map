## 1. Auction Phase (shared — Declarer & Defender)

Run during the bidding, every hand, regardless of whether you're bidding.

### Listen & anticipate
- **Don't wait until your turn — think during every bid** (LJ, AIB, EK)
- **Convert each bid into distribution + HCP for the bidder** (LJ, KW, AIB)
- Note what is *asked* vs *not asked* (e.g. no major bid = likely no 4-card major) (AIB, KW)
- Track shape information being described by both opponents (AIB, KW)
- Recognise the system tools they're using (Stayman, transfers, NMF, 4SF, cuebids) (AIB, EK)

### Build the picture
- **Form a mental picture of one opponent's hand pattern** (KW, LJ, BTU)
- Start with the simplest assumption (minimum length promised by opening / response) (KW)
- Note shape constraints from negative inferences (1H–1S–1NT → opener is 2-5-3-3 / 3-5-3-2 / 3-5-2-3) (KW)
- Add your HCP + dummy's (when visible) + declarer's estimated HCP, subtract from 40 → partner's range (EK, LC)

### Defender-specific auction work
- Think about what you'd lead during the bidding, not after (LJ)
- Watch for lead-directing double opportunities on artificial bids (Stayman, transfers, Blackwood responses) (EK)
- Note surprises by partner or declarer in point-count / distribution (LJ)

### Declarer-specific auction work
- Review opponents' bidding (or lack of it) before you play to trick one (BTU, KW, BTFY = "R" in CRASH)
- Don't waste energy second-guessing your own bidding (BTU)

## 2. Declarer Analysis (pre-trick-1)

Run after dummy comes down, before you call a card from dummy.

**Discipline:**
- **Pause at least 10 seconds before playing from dummy** (KW, USBF, AR, BTU)
- Treat it like a play problem on paper handed to you by a friend (BTU)

### Step 1 — Count
- **NT: count winners (sure tricks)** (KW, LO, AR, LC, BBO, BTFY)
- **Suit: count losers** (KW, LO, LC, BBO)
- Then cross-check by counting the other (winners in suit / losers in NT) (KW, BW)
- Count quick losers separately from total potential losers (LO)
- Focus count on a "master hand" (long-trump hand in 5-3/6-2; stronger hand in 4-4) (KW)

### Step 2 — Gap
- Work out how many extra tricks you need (AR, LC)
- How many tricks can defenders cash when they get in? (KW, LO, LC)

### Step 3 — Source of extra tricks
- **Identify source of tricks for each suit** (LO, BTFY = "S" in CRASH, BW)
- Three mechanisms (NT): Force (knock out higher cards), Length (exhaust opponents), Position/Finesse (AR)
- Suit-contract extras: ruff losers in dummy, side-suit setup, finesse, throw-in / endplay (LC, BW, KW)
- Consider: dummy reversal, cross-ruff, loser-on-loser, squeeze, trump coup (BW)

### Step 4 — Hazards & control
- **Identify the danger hand — which defender must be kept off lead?** (KW, LO)
- Count stoppers / controls (LO)
- Plan hold-ups to sever defensive communication (LO)
- Predict which suit opponents will attack when they get in (KW)

### Step 5 — Trump management (suit contracts)
- **Draw trumps now or postpone?** (KW, LC, LO)
- If natural winners ≥ tricks needed → draw trumps first (KW)
- If ruffs needed in dummy → set up before drawing (KW)
- Count opponents' trumps as you go (KW)

### Step 6 — Communication & entries
- **Plan entries between hands** (KW, LO, BW)
- Cash short-suit winners last if needed as entries (KW)
- Unblock high cards; decide which hand to lead from for each suit (KW)

### Step 7 — Read the lead
- **Analyse the opening lead** (KW, BBO, BTFY = "A" in CRASH)
- Length or shortness? Honor combination implied? (KW)

### Step 8 — Honor combinations & order of play
- Assess best play in each suit, fit with communication, revise plan (LO)
- Decide which suit to attack first (KW, LC)
- "First question: what can the position be? Then: of those, which can I cope with?" (BW / Barden)

### Step 9 — Think like a defender
- What do defenders know about my hand? (KW)
- What suit will they lead / discard? (KW)

## 3. Defender Analysis (pre-trick-1)

Run when dummy comes down, before you play to trick 1 (or as the leader, before selecting your lead).

**Discipline:**
- **Don't rush, don't get rushed, especially trick 1** (LJ, EK)
- Plan your defense sooner rather than later (LJ)
- Have a reason for what you do (LJ)

### Step 1 — Count the points
- **Add declarer's HCP + dummy's HCP, subtract from 40 → what you + partner hold** (EK, LC)
- Divide the missing points based on the auction (LC)
- When dummy looks strong, assign declarer the minimum for their bid (EK)

### Step 2 — Picture the unseen hands
- **Convert each bid into shape + HCP** (LJ, KW)
- Memorise the original layout of your hand + dummy (LJ, KW)
- Build a hand-pattern picture for declarer (3-5-3-2 etc.) (KW)
- Memorise common patterns (4432, 4333, 4441, 5332, 5431, 6322, 7321) (KW)

### Step 3 — Count declarer's tricks
- **Count three things: declarer's distribution, HCP, and tricks** (LJ / Phil Read)
- Are there apparent tricks for declarer's side suits? If not, look to trump tricks (overruff, uppercut, ruff-and-sluff promotion) (EK)

### Step 4 — Identify winning defense
- "See" the layouts where the contract can be defeated — identify the 2-3 declarer hand-patterns where you can beat it, before playing to trick 1 (LJ)
- For each likely declarer pattern, work out the required defense (LJ)
- Know what suit declarer fears (LJ, EK)
- Knowing why declarer is doing X helps you decide why you should be doing Y (EK)

### Step 5 — Active or passive?
- **Decide: active (cash/set up tricks fast) or passive (don't break suits, don't help declarer)?** (LJ, LC, EK)
- Balanced declarer hand → passive (EK)
- Long-suit declarer / source of tricks visible → active (EK)
- Don't make an attacking play unless bidding/dummy supports it (LC)
- Don't prematurely grab aces — aces capture kings (LC)

### Step 6 — Opening lead (if you're on lead)
- **Avoid suits opponents bid; consider unbid suits** (BWO, EK)
- 4th best from long suits vs NT (BWO)
- Top of solid / interior sequence (BWO)
- K from AK, top of doubleton vs suit (BWO)
- Don't underlead an ace vs suit contracts (BWO)
- Consider trump lead when auction suggests cross-ruff (BWO, EK)
- Avoid trump lead when opponents are misfitted (EK)
- Avoid singleton trump leads (EK)
- Lead partner's bid suit unless reason not to (BWO)
- Vs small slam: don't cash unsupported ace unless probable second trick (BWO)
- Vs grand slam: safe lead, often trump (BWO)

### Step 7 — Memory & watching
- Notice + commit to memory the first few cards partner and declarer play (LJ)
- If specific low cards in 1-2 suits will matter, decide now to watch those spot cards (LJ)
- Watch partner's signals — they show length + possible HCP (KW)

### Step 8 — Inferences from partner
- If partner didn't lead your suit: partner is void / leading singleton / has the ace fearing declarer's king / forgot the bidding (EK)
- Collect legitimate clues from partner's actions (LJ)
